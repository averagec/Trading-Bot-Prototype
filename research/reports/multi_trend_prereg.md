## 1. Question

Does a long/short, trailing-return-sign rule on weekly-rebalanced D1 positions, sized to equal ex-ante risk and balanced across asset classes (FX, equity indices, energy, metals, agriculture), earn a net-of-cost, net-of-assumed-financing return per unit of risk that exceeds what the same sizing, turnover, long/short bias and costs earn when the sign path carries no timing information (the block-permuted null)? And does that result survive removal of any single asset class, of its three best years, and half of the eras?

**Primary estimand, per variant v:** SR_v = the annualised net Sharpe ratio of the weekly P&L (`mean / sd * sqrt(52)`) of the class-balanced USD portfolio, spread and commission charged, **financing S0 = none** (the part measurable here). Two stressed versions, S1 and S2 (section 4), enter the gates. No cash interest is credited (excess-return convention). The *timing* hypothesis is tested at S0 against the null (financing depends on |weight| and not on the sign, so it barely differs between actual and null; section 4).
Prior (stated, not tested): FX-class momentum is weak; a diversified result would have to come from the other classes. Equity indices, oil and metals trend premia in the literature are mostly in samples before 2009.

## 2. Data

### 2.1 Universe by rule (fixed now; applied mechanically by `python -m research.multi_trend_data screen`)

* **R1 candidates:** every symbol with a file `research/data_multi/<SYMBOL>_D1.parquet` at the freeze. The universe is *today's broker list*: survivorship (instruments the broker still offers, which were selected by the broker and by liquidity) is a caveat that cannot be removed here and is stated in the report.
* **R2 class** (`classify`): path `Forex` with both currencies in the G8 set (USD EUR GBP JPY CHF CAD AUD NZD) = **FX** (the 28 pairs); `Cash*` paths: name containing an energy token (OIL, BRENT, WTI, NGAS ...) = **EN**, a metal token = **MT**, otherwise **EQ** (indices); `Metals` path = MT; `Agriculture` and unmatched `Commodities` = **AG**; `Commodities`/`Energy` names by the token lists. Anything unrecognised is excluded and listed for the owner (`OTHER`).
* **R3 excluded by class, by written rule:** (a) **Crypto** and **single-stock CFDs**: survivorship/selection and short histories (as instructed). (b) **Exotics** (Forex pairs outside G8, `Exotics` path): excluded. Reasons fixed before looking at returns: their trend profits are dominated by carry and devaluation episodes (TRY, ZAR, MXN, RUB), whose broker financing is unknown, large, sign-dependent and cannot be bracketed by one unsigned S1/S2 charge; spreads are several times the majors' and the recorded D1 spread is not credible; some are pegged (HKD, CNH, SAR), so a vol-targeted position has unbounded leverage in a zero-vol regime. Including them would make the headline number depend on assumptions that cannot be tested. No result is reported for them. (c) **DXY / USD index CFDs:** excluded; the dollar index is a fixed linear combination of the FX majors, so it is double counting, and its history is short.
* **R4 history (entry):** an instrument may trade from the first Wednesday on which it has at least **270** cleaned D1 bars behind it (the longest look-back is 365 calendar days = 261 weekdays, plus the 7-day staleness tolerance), and not before 2003-01-01. Earlier history is warm-up only (the same convention as A2'/R3-1: 2000-2002 spreads are nominal constants).
* **R5 minimum live span N = 8 years:** the span from entry to 2021-06-01 must be at least 8.0 years (entry no later than mid-2013). Reason: shorter spans add few weeks, no era coverage and a composition shift (today's survivors with short histories entering in the late sample); 8 years is 43 % of DEV and covers at least two of the three eras. Instruments with `0 < span < 8` are listed as excluded for span only. The rule is applied to DEV history only (never to FINAL).
* **R6 data failures (mechanical, `multi_trend_data.py`):** an instrument is excluded if (i) its extreme days show a **roll-like** pattern (at least 20 days with |z| > 5, at least half of them in one 5-day-of-month bin, and a binomial tail for that concentration, times 6, below 0.001; revised before any run, see the log), or (ii) more than **30 %** of its live calendar years are **masked**, or (iii) it is a **duplicate** (weekly return correlation at least 0.98 on at least 100 common weeks with an instrument already kept; the longer history wins, ties alphabetical).
* **R7 masks (flat, not excluded):** a calendar year is masked for an instrument (flat for every week starting in that year) if its weekday coverage is below 80 %, more than 25 % of its returns are exactly zero, a run of 8 identical closes occurs, or a hole of at least 8 consecutive missing weekdays starts or ends in it (both years). Real shocks (SNB 2015-01-15, the 2020 crash) are **not** masked and **not** treated as errors: a jump is only removed if it is a reverting one-bar spike (|r| above 10 robust sigma and 5 %, reversed by at least 65 % on the next bar), a data-error repair that looks one bar ahead and is reported by count (`clean_d1`); non-positive prices are dropped. Masks are tested not to look at P/L.
* **R8 FX placeholder:** the 50-point spread is a sentinel (missing) only for FX in 2019-01..03 and 2020-05..2021-09; elsewhere a recorded value is a quote (nominal pre-2010 constants included).
* **V0 viability gate (new, fixed now):** the DEV run is recommended only if the frozen universe has **at least three classes with at least three instruments each and at least 25 instruments in total**. If not, the premise of the study (breadth across asset classes) is not available in this broker's history, and the owner must decide before any run whether to continue as an FX-plus-few study (power section 8 shows that breadth does not raise power at fixed T). **The author reports the V0 status with the universe and does not run DEV.**
* **Universe freeze:** `research/output/multi_trend_dev/universe.csv` with `universe_sha256.txt`; the counts per class and the V0 status are written into the log at the end of this file. Any change to the universe after the first DEV run is forbidden; any change before it is logged.

### 2.2 Bars, clocks, conventions (fixed)

* **Source:** broker D1 bars, label time = broker server day (NY-close convention, UTC+2/UTC+3), UTC-labelled; one bar per weekday. Weekend-labelled bars are merged into the next Monday bar (first open, extreme high/low, last close). A bar labelled d closes at the end of server day d.
* **Calendar (common, weekly):** decision date D_k = every **Wednesday** (arbitrary, frozen; mid-week to avoid weekend and Friday/Monday liquidity; the other four weekdays are descriptive and may not be selected from). **Signal from bars with label ≤ D_k only** (the bar closed at the server midnight ending D_k; if that instrument has no bar that day, the last earlier bar if at most 3 days old, else flat and counted). **Execution at the open of the first bar dated after D_k** (Thursday; if absent, the next bar within 4 days; else flat and counted). The position set at k is held to the execution price of k+1; no intra-week action. A missing exit execution is marked at the last available close before the next scheduled execution (P&L never zeroed) and the instrument is flat for the next period only.
* **Look-backs are calendar-day:** price at D_k versus price at the last bar at or before D_k - L days (at most 7 days stale), L in {30, 91, 182, 365}; this removes the dependence of the look-back on each market's holiday calendar. Volatility is the std of the last 60 D1 close-to-close log returns (at least 40), times sqrt(260).
* **Return convention (USD account, CFD, constant equity base, additive weekly returns):** for an instrument quoted in currency Q the USD return on notional between executions is `r_local x Q1/Q0` where r_local = P1/P0 - 1 and Q is the USD value of one unit of the quote currency (derivation: profit N(P1-P0) in Q converted at the exit rate, divided by the USD notional N P0 Q0 at entry). For USD-quoted instruments the factor is 1; for USD-base FX pairs it reproduces R3-1's `1 - P0/P1`; for EUR, GBP, AUD, NZD, JPY, CAD and CHF quotes Q comes from the D1 open of the corresponding G8 pair at the same executions (`CONV_PAIRS`); for other quote currencies (for example HKD, which is pegged) the local return is used and the count is reported. The difference to the local return is the second-order cross-term r x (Q1/Q0 - 1) (a 3 % weekly move times a 1 % weekly currency move is 0.03 %; the identity and the bound are unit-tested); **local-currency returns are reported as a descriptive variant.** Conversion pairs are used as price series only, not as tradable instruments, and need not be in the universe.
* **Spread field:** the recorded D1 `spread` (points) is one number per bar of unknown timing; a class floor and a trailing median are used (section 4); the field is never used as a feature or signal.

## 3. Family: five variants, written down now (K = 5)

Signal for instrument i at k: sign s of the log return from D_k - L (calendar days) to the signal bar; 0 if exactly zero or unavailable; always invested (no flat band).

| Id | rule |
|---|---|
| M1 | L = 30 days (1 month) |
| M3 | L = 91 days (3 months) |
| M6 | L = 182 days (6 months) |
| M12 | L = 365 days (12 months, no skip) |
| B4 | the mean of the four signs (fractional position in {-1, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 1}): the diversified-lookback blend; a position is smaller where horizons disagree |

Textbook horizons only; no skip-month forms (they belong to cross-sectional equity momentum and R3-1 already showed the 12-1 and 6-1 forms add nothing visible); no band, threshold, ensemble other than B4, monthly rebalance or carry/value term. B4 is a member of the family, not a separate hypothesis.
**Sizing (all history-only):**
1. Per-instrument stand-alone risk: `volw_i = min(3, 0.10 / sigma_i)`, sigma_i the 60-day annualised vol.
2. **Class-balanced weights (primary):** `w_i = s_i x volw_i / n_c x g_c / C`, where n_c is the number of tradable instruments of i's class at k, C the number of tradable classes, and `g_c = min(4, 0.10 / vol_c)` with vol_c the ex-ante (120-day) annualised vol of the class's equal-weight *always-long* basket of vol-scaled returns. g_c needs no signs, so it is identical in the actual run and in every null replicate, and it equalises class risk: without it 28 correlated FX pairs would carry far more risk than two oil contracts. Each class then contributes the same ex-ante risk (for an all-long book); the risk of the signed book is whatever it is, and is reported.
3. **Equal-risk weighting** (`w_i = s_i volw_i / N_k`) is the descriptive alternative. No correlation model, no portfolio-level vol target (Sharpe is scale free); realised annual volatility, mean gross notional and turnover are reported, and the leverage that would give 10 % portfolio vol is `10 % / realised vol`. A portfolio vol cap is a deployment matter (FTMO drawdown rules) and not part of the DEV estimand.
4. Benchmark BL: always long every instrument, same sizing, weekday, costs and financing. Reported, not a DEV gate (it is mostly equity and gold drift); its role is the null (the null keeps the long/short bias) and the FINAL condition.

## 4. Costs and financing (assumptions fixed now; none is data)

* **Spread:** half the spread is paid on every change of position: `cost = |w_k - w_(k-1)| x (0.5 x spread_frac + 0.25 bp)`. `spread_frac` = max(trailing median of the clean recorded D1 spread x point / price, class floor), causal (bars up to the decision bar). **Class floors (full spread, bp of price):** FX 1.5, equity index 3, energy 8, metals 5, agriculture 15. The floors are *assumptions* (typical retail CFD spreads on liquid instruments; the recorded D1 field is zero on a large share of days and constant before 2010), chosen to be conservative rather than precise. Recorded values above the floor are used. The 50-point FX sentinel is treated as missing (R8). Stress: spread x2. Commission 0.25 bp per side of weight traded (the FX account's $2.50 per lot; zero on most CFDs, so conservative).
* **Financing / swap: unknown.** `swap_long`, `swap_short` and `swap_mode` in the spec files are *today's snapshot* (rates were near zero for a decade and 5 % in 2023; modes differ across symbols), so they are not used as a DEV cost; they are listed in the data-quality report for the owner. Explicit stress charges per year on gross notional, unsigned (every position pays; the differential sign is not available):

| class | S0 | S1 | S2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| FX | 0 | 1.5 % | 3.5 % |
| equity index | 0 | 2.0 % | 4.0 % |
| energy | 0 | 2.5 % | 5.0 % |
| metals | 0 | 3.0 % | 5.0 % |
| agriculture | 0 | 3.0 % | 5.0 % |

  FX, metals: as R3-1. **Cash-index CFD financing includes a dividend adjustment:** a long pays interest on notional and receives the dividend; a short pays the dividend and receives interest. Over 2003 to 2021 the net for longs was close to zero or a credit and for shorts a charge of the dividend yield plus the broker mark-up, so an unsigned 2 % charge is hostile for longs and roughly right for shorts. Futures-style CFDs (energy, agriculture, `.c` symbols) usually carry no swap but the roll is paid or earned in the price series (contango costs a long; the broker may apply a roll adjustment); the unsigned charge stands in for it.
  S1 is a central plausible cost, S2 a hostile case. The charge depends on |w| and not on the sign path, so it is nearly identical in the actual run and in every null replicate: the timing test (G1) is run at S0, and the question "does it survive financing" is carried by G2, G3 and G5 at S1. **The break-even charge c*** (the annual charge on gross notional at which mean net P&L is zero) is reported per variant as a re-expression for when the owner's swap table is known; the table is a disclosed input to the FINAL report, never to DEV selection. A verdict at S0 alone is never used.

## 5. Null (fixed; replicates the whole selection procedure)

* **Block-permuted sign null with a derangement, as R3-1 revision 1.** The signal matrix of each variant (weeks x instruments, computed once from the real data) is cut into blocks of 26 weeks; the blocks are permuted by a random **derangement** (no block stays in place) and re-concatenated. Each instrument's sign path keeps its persistence inside a block, its long/short bias and its flip rate; the timing relation to the next period's return is removed. Sizing (vol and class scale at each real date), the live set, spreads, returns, execution, conversion, costs and financing are recomputed at the original dates exactly as in the real run.
* **Unbalanced panel:** instruments enter at different dates. Instruments are grouped into **entry cohorts by entry year** (clipped to 2003..2013); each cohort has its own derangement over the weeks from its first tradable week to the end, shared by its members (cross-sectional alignment inside a cohort is kept, alignment across cohorts is not, stated; the dominant cohort is the full-span one). A block moved to weeks where an instrument was not yet tradable carries a zero signal (flat), which slightly lowers the null's exposure; the null's mean turnover and mean gross notional relative to the actual run are written to the output (`null_turnover_ratio`, `null_gross_ratio`) so the direction of this imperfection can be read.
* The same permutation is applied to all five variants in a replicate, so the joint null of the five Sharpe ratios is available. **M = 2,000 replicates, seed 20261104**; the smallest attainable p-value is 1/2,001. Individual p_v = (1 + #{null SR_v >= SR_v}) / (1 + M).
* **What the null tests:** that the *timing* of the sign path carries no information beyond its long/short bias; a mostly-long trend sign in a drifting asset is not credited. Known imperfections, direction stated: a block moved to a later place may carry a sign computed from returns overlapping its new place (raises null SR, conservative); the joins add a few extra flips (raises null cost, liberal); the loss of cross-cohort alignment reduces the null's common-factor risk (raises null SR a little, conservative).
* **Secondary, descriptive nulls:** (i) circular same-lag shift inside each cohort (lags 78 to K-78 weeks, every 4th); (ii) an independent derangement per instrument. If either contradicts the primary result for the chosen candidate it is called fragile; neither can change eligibility.
* **Multiplicity (decision):** the family is the K = 5 variants. The adjusted p-values are the **Westfall-Young step-down maxT** on null-standardised Sharpe ratios (z_v = (SR_v - null mean)/null sd), using the joint replicates, so the dependence between the five variants (they overlap strongly) is respected; family alpha 0.05 (DEV). **Holm on the individual p_v is reported alongside** and is never smaller. *Deviation from R3-1, stated:* R3-1 required Holm over the individual p-values and a single-step max p; with K = 5 and substantial overlap, the Holm threshold of 0.01 on the first step is the Bonferroni bound and wastes power the dependence structure returns, so the dependence-aware step-down is the decision rule and Holm is the conservative cross-check. The campaign-wide error is larger than 0.05 (this arm follows several looks at overlapping FX data) and is stated, not corrected; GAMEPLAN allows two FINAL candidates in total at alpha 0.025.
* **Bootstrap (reporting only; no gate uses it):** circular block bootstrap of the weekly net P&L, whole weeks of all instruments together, blocks of 13, 26 and 52 weeks, B = 5,000 each, seed 20261103; the quoted interval is the one with the lowest lower bound. Also on the same resamples: S0/S1/S2, the difference to BL, each variant without each class, and each class alone.

## 6. Gates and decision table (fixed)

A variant is **eligible** only if all hold on the primary run (Wednesday, class-balanced, USD conversion, all unmasked years; S1 unless stated):

| Gate | Condition |
|---|---|
| **G1 timing** | Westfall-Young step-down adjusted p (S0 Sharpe against the block-permuted null) <= 0.05 |
| **G2 survives financing** | SR_v(S1) > 0 (point) |
| **G3a** | SR_v(S1) > 0 after removing the three best calendar years by S1 net P&L |
| **G3b** | S1 net P&L positive in at least **12 of the 18** calendar years 2003 to 2020 (2021 is partial and reported). R3-1 used 13; 12 is the expectation for a true Sharpe near 0.5 (P(year > 0) = Phi(0.5) = 0.69, 12.4 of 18), so 13 would reject half of the effects the study is built to find; false-positive control is G1's job, not G3b's (a coin-flip passes 12 of 18 with probability 0.12) |
| **G3c** | SR_v(S1) > 0 in at least 2 of the 3 eras (2003-2009, 2010-2013, 2014-2021-05) |
| **G4 no class carries it** | for every class in the universe, SR_v(S1) > 0 with that class removed (weights re-balanced over the remaining classes) |
| **G5 materiality** | SR_v(S1) >= 0.30 |

Descriptive, never gates: the four other weekdays, one-day execution delay, spread x2, equal-risk weighting, local-currency returns, starts 2010 and 2014, without 2008, without 2020, each class alone, S2, SR minus BL, the circular-shift and per-instrument nulls. A primary result that flips sign on a different weekday or under local-currency returns is called fragile.
**Selection:** among eligible variants the one with the highest SR_v(S1) is **the frozen candidate**, written to `research/output/multi_trend_dev/frozen_candidate.json` with this file's sha256. If none is eligible there is no candidate and FINAL is not used. Nothing is chosen by looking at FINAL.

## 8. Power (stated before running)

Analytic plug-in (normal approximation, SE(SR) = sqrt((1 + SR^2/2) x f / T), T years, f the variance inflation for autocorrelation, shown at f = 1.0 and 1.3; true Sharpe means the S0 Sharpe; the wording of R3-1):

| Window | T (years) | one-sided alpha | critical SR | power at true SR 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 | SR for 80 % power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DEV 2003 to 2021-05, one test | 18.5 | 0.05 | 0.38 / 0.44 | 0.36 / 0.31 | 0.68 / 0.59 | 0.92 / 0.85 | 0.99 / 0.96 | 0.60 / 0.68 |
| DEV, five-variant family at 0.01 (Bonferroni bound for the step-down) | 18.5 | 0.01 | 0.54 / 0.62 | 0.16 / 0.12 | 0.43 / 0.34 | 0.79 / 0.67 | 0.95 / 0.88 | 0.76 / 0.88 |
| FINAL 2021-06 to 2025-04, one candidate | 3.9 | 0.025 | 0.99 / 1.13 | 0.09 / 0.08 | 0.18 / 0.15 | 0.34 / 0.28 | 0.51 / 0.43 | 1.6 / 1.9 |

Honest reading:
* **Breadth does not change the standard error of a portfolio's Sharpe ratio.** SE depends on the number of years, not on the number of instruments. Breadth raises the *true* portfolio Sharpe (many weakly trending independent drifts add in quadrature), it does not shorten the sample. The DEV test is powered for a true S0 Sharpe of about **0.6 to 0.9**, not for the 0.3 to 0.5 that the post-2009 literature would suggest. At a true S0 Sharpe of 0.4, the chance that the first step-down rejects is about 25 to 50 %, and joint eligibility (G1 to G5, where G5 needs S1 >= 0.30 which at S1 costs of 0.2 to 0.4 Sharpe means S0 near 0.6 or more) is lower still. The expected DEV outcome is "no eligible variant" even if a modest edge exists.
* The empirical null width in R3-1 was wider than the iid standard error (persistent signs have few independent bets); the synthetic calibration on the real calendar and universe shape measures this directly (`calibration.json`: `sr0_best_sd` at strength 0, and the pass probabilities against realised Sharpe).
