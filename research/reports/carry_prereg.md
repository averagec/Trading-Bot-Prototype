## 1. Question and estimand

Does a monthly-rebalanced, volatility-targeted, dollar-neutral cross-currency portfolio of the ten G10 currencies, formed on lagged interbank interest rates (alone, magnitude-weighted, or combined with 12-1 momentum), earn a net excess return per unit of risk that exceeds what the same sizing, turnover, gross exposure and costs earn (i) when the ranking is attached to the wrong currencies, and (ii) when the direction of the positions is random?

**Primary estimand, per variant v:** SR_v = the annualised Sharpe ratio of the monthly net excess return `mean / sd x sqrt(12)` (USD cash is the benchmark), net of spread and commission, with interest accrual credited at the interbank rate and **zero broker mark-up (S0)**. S0 is the question "does the premium exist"; S1 and S2 (broker mark-up) answer "can this broker pass it on" (section 5). Reported next to it: the split of S0 into the accrual part and the spot part (the spot part is what UIP says should offset the accrual).
**Plain statement of what is and is not being tested:** the accrual is almost deterministic given the rates; the statistical question is whether spot movements offset it. A positive realised Sharpe that the nulls and the bootstrap cannot separate from zero is the outcome **"not detectable at DEV power"**, never "carry works".

## 2. Data, universe, handling (fixed)

* **Universe:** USD (numeraire), EUR, GBP, JPY, AUD, CAD, CHF, NZD, NOK, SEK, through the nine USD pairs EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, AUDUSD, USDCAD, USDCHF, NZDUSD, USDNOK, USDSEK (D1, broker server day, `research/data_multi`). Crosses are not used (long data holes; a currency portfolio is implementable through USD pairs; `carry_data_notes.md` section 5). The USD position is the negative sum of the others and is implicit (its rate enters the ranking). A missing pair file refuses the run: no subset, no substitution. **USDNOK starts on 2006-02-06 (3,960 DEV bars; the other eight start in 2000-03).** Decision (pre-run note 1): the universe stays the ten currencies, and **NOK enters the panel when it has the history every variant needs (252 bars of momentum, 80 bars of covariance): first live decision 2007-01-31, first holding period February 2007.** The first 49 of the 221 periods therefore have nine live currencies (k = 3 of 9), the rest ten (k = 3 of 10). Why not drop NOK for the whole sample: it is one of the two exotic-ish currencies the gates are about and a high-yield one for part of the sample, its removal would be a choice made after seeing that the data start late, and the gates already contain the nine-currency answer (G4a drops NOK in turn, G4b drops NOK and SEK). Why not start the study in 2007: it would discard a quarter of DEV and the 2003-2006 high-dispersion years. Why not back-fill NOK from a cross: EURNOK starts in 2013 and has holes. Consequences, all handled: the live flag is identical for all four constructions; the weights stay dollar-neutral on the live set; the permutation null relabels only live currencies (`permute_weights`, tested); the entry of NOK in February 2007 is one extra change of the ranking universe and is charged like any other trade; a descriptive sensitivity `from_2007-02_all_ten_live` is reported, and the leave-NOK-out run is in the per-currency table. The Sharpe of the first 49 periods is not comparable in cross-section breadth with the rest, and that is stated, not corrected. Survivorship in the choice of currencies (the ten that are liquid and still traded today) is a caveat; NOK and SEK are the least liquid.
* **Rates:** FRED/OECD 3-month interbank, monthly average, percent per year (`research/data_multi/rates`). **Lag: the decision at the end of calendar month m reads month m-1** (a lag below 1 is refused by the code). A missing month is carried forward at most 3 months (USA 2020-04 is the only gap in DEV). Details and caveats: `carry_data_notes.md`. **Publication lag of the source:** FRED/OECD publishes with a delay of weeks (the 2026-11-03 fetch had USA to 2026-09 and JPN to 2026-08), so a decision "at the end of month m" cannot be reproduced from this source with lag 1. Lag 1 is not look-ahead (the month m-1 average is computable from public daily fixings at that date and is on average 1.5 months old), and the rankings are persistent; but the **lag-2 result is reported next to every lag-1 result** (`SR_S0_lag2`, `SR_S1_lag2` in `variants.csv`, `lag2_SR_S1` in `summary.json`, and the sensitivity table) as a stated robustness line. It is not a gate and cannot change a category.
* **Bars:** weekend bars dropped (counted); zero and 50-point-placeholder spreads cleaned by `research.ai.dataset.add_clean_spread` (convention of A2'/R3-1); the cost spread is defined in section 5.
* **Window:** holding periods from 2003-01-01 to 2021-05-31: 221 monthly periods (18 full calendar years 2003 to 2020 plus Jan to May 2021). Price history from 2000-03 and rate history before 2003 are look-back only. Eras (as A2'/R3-1): E1 2003-2009, E2 2010-2013, E3 2014-2021-05.
* **Calendar:** decision date = last weekday of each calendar month (first 2002-12-31, last 2021-05-31); signal uses bars with date <= the decision date; execution = **open of the first D1 bar after the decision date**; the period ends at the next execution (the last period at the close of the last DEV bar). No fill at the signal bar's own close. The period belongs to the calendar year of its execution date.
* **Sizing:** ex-ante annualised volatility of the portfolio = sqrt(w' S w) where S = covariance of the nine daily currency-vs-USD log returns over the last 120 D1 bars (at least 80) ending at the decision bar, times 260. The scale applied to the unit-gross weights is `min(4, 0.10 / vol)`; a currency whose covariance is unavailable is not live. Rebalance every month (the monthly change in the scale is traded and charged). Sharpe is scale-free, including the mark-up (which is proportional to gross notional).
* **Return convention:** currency c against USD, X = USD per unit of c; P&L of a weight w (fraction of constant equity, additive monthly returns, no compounding) = `w x ((X1/X0 - 1) + accrual)`; USD-base pairs use X = 1/P. Prices are the recorded bid. Drift of weights within the month is ignored.
* **Live set:** a currency is live at a decision if its latest bar is at most 5 days old, its lagged rate and the USD rate exist, its covariance and 12-1 momentum exist, and it is not in `OFF_MONTHS` (empty; to be set only by a pre-run note from data screens). Fewer than 6 live currencies means flat. k (the number on each side) = round(0.3 n): 3 for n = 10, 3 for 9, 2 for 8. Nothing in the live set looks at the future (tested).

## 3. Strategy family: K = 3 candidates and one control (written now)

All four are dollar-neutral at the currency level (weights over the ten currencies sum to zero, gross 2 before scaling) and are decided only from information at the decision date.

| Id | Construction | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** carry-rank | +1/k on the k highest, -1/k on the k lowest lagged 3-month rates (ties: last month's position kept, then fixed order) | rate level |
| **V2** carry-continuous | weight proportional to (rate - cross-sectional mean of the live rates), scaled to gross 2 | rate level, magnitude-weighted |
| **V3** carry + momentum | rank by the sum of the cross-sectional z-score of the lagged rate and the z-score of the 12-1 momentum, +1/k top, -1/k bottom | rate and price |
| **M** control (outside the family, never a candidate) | same ranking on 12-1 momentum alone | price only |

12-1 momentum of currency c = log of the c-vs-USD D1 close 21 bars ago minus the same 252 bars ago (own bar count); USD = 0; the cross-section is centred on the live mean. Benchmark BL (report only): long all nine foreign currencies against USD, equal weights, same sizing and costs.
**Not run, by decision:** a "crash filter" (volatility or drawdown cut). The 2008 crash is known to me, so any such filter would be fitted to a known event, and the volatility targeting is already the only risk control. No other window, lag, k, band, ensemble, weekday or universe is a result of this study; descriptive sensitivities (rate lag 2, top/bottom 2 and 4, USD left out of the ranking, start 2010 and 2014, to 2019, without 2008-09, spread x2 and x4, mark-up S2) are produced and cannot add or remove eligibility. M is in the report precisely because FX momentum is expected to fail. **M never changes a category or an eligibility and can never be a candidate; it is reported with its p-values.** If M passes its null the report says so and points to the calibration (a pass occurs in about 5 % of draws, and more when currency returns are persistent or crash-exposed, section 8); no discretionary reading follows from it.
**Tie-break (arbitrary, fixed, tested):** among exactly equal scores the currency that was long last month ranks higher and the one that was short ranks lower (positions are kept); any remaining tie is broken by the fixed currency order USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, AUD, CAD, CHF, NZD, NOK, SEK (lower index higher). Earlier version: ties by currency order alone, which short-biased the last currencies in the list (SEK, NOK); on the real DEV rates exact ties across the long/short boundary occur at about 2 decisions of 222 (rates only), so the effect is small, but the rule is now position-keeping.
**Why two carry constructions:** the rank rule ignores the size of the differential (robust when rates are tied near zero, as in 2009-2015 and 2020-21), the continuous rule uses it. V1 and V2 are strongly correlated; the effective number of independent variants is about 2.

## 4. Accrual (fixed; details and hand-check in `carry_data_notes.md` section 2 and the tests)

Every calendar day in `[ED_j, ED_(j+1))` (ED = the next business day after the decision date) accrues `w x (r_c/B_c - r_USD/B_USD)` with r the monthly-average rate of the calendar month of that day, B = 365 for GBP, AUD, CAD, NZD and 360 otherwise (USD 360). Weekends and the Wednesday triple are the same days allocated differently; the sum over a year is 365 days. The **realised** month's average is used for the accrual (it is P&L, not a decision input; the decision uses the lagged month). The 3-month rate is an overnight proxy (credit and term components, mainly 2007-2012). Interest on collateral is not credited (excess return). On 1 January and some holidays the first broker bar is a day after the nominal execution date (9 to 12 of 222 decisions per pair): the accrual of that period then starts and ends one day early; immaterial, stated.

## 5. Costs and financing (fixed)

* **Spread:** at each execution a change of weight pays `|dW| x 0.5 x hs`, hs = max(trailing 20-bar median of the cleaned recorded D1 spread in points x point / price, **floor 1.0 bp of notional for the seven large currencies and 4.0 bp for NOK and SEK**). The floor exists because the recorded D1 field is a placeholder in many years (zero on 55 to 68 % of bars in 2016-17, a median of 1 point in 2013-14, 2018 and 2020-21; `carry_data_notes.md` 4.1); the floors are assumptions, not data (in the loaded series the median cost spread is the floor for EUR, GBP, NOK and SEK and 1.2 to 3.0 bp for the other five pairs). The recorded fields of USDSEK (750 points in 2010-12, 12 in 2013) and USDNOK (395 in 2013-14) swing by factors of 30 to 60 and are not market spreads. The cleaning repairs zeros from same-year or whole-window medians, so a repaired cost depends on later bars: it touches costs only, never a weight. Stress: x2 and x4. The MT5 D1 `spread` is one recorded value per bar and is not shown to be the open or the rollover value, which is another reason the floor is an assumption. **Commission:** 0.25 bp per unit of weight traded (live account: $2.50 per lot per side).
* **Financing / broker mark-up (the decisive uncertainty).** The broker's swap includes a mark-up that applies to both directions: the AUDUSD snapshot implies about 2.2 % a year of notional (4.61 points per lot per day against an implied interest differential of 1.0), i.e. several times that pair's interest differential. **All nine tables are now read (pre-run note 1): implied mark-up 0.96 to 2.71 % a year of notional, mean 1.72, median 1.60** (EUR 1.43, GBP 1.29, JPY 2.71, AUD 2.17, CAD 1.80, CHF 2.35, NZD 1.19, NOK 0.96, SEK 1.60; percentages at the 2021-05-31 price; today's snapshot, not history). Treatment: charge `m x sum|W| x days/365` each period, unsigned (every position pays); **S0 m = 0 (interbank accrual, the primary estimand); S1 m = 1.5 % a year (gate level; just under the snapshot median of 1.60, below five of the nine pairs; raised from 1.0 % by pre-run revision 1); S2 m = 2.5 % a year (above every pair except JPY)**. A per-pair stress with the nine snapshot numbers (`markup_snapshot_per_pair`) is a descriptive sensitivity. S1 was first fixed at 1.0 % when only the AUDUSD table had been seen; the other eight tables were read afterwards and the lead decided to raise S1 to 1.5 % before any run (pre-run revision 1, Log). The reason is the swap table, not any return: no return of any rule or pair has been computed or seen. Because the mark-up is proportional to gross notional and almost identical in the real run and in every null replicate, it does not enter the nulls; it enters G2, G3, G4 and G5 through the S1 series. The break-even mark-up (the m at which mean net P&L is zero) is reported for every variant. A unit-gross carry portfolio earns of the order of 2 to 3 % a year of accrual per 2 units of gross notional, so **at S1 (1.5 %) roughly three quarters of the income and at S2 all or more than all of it is consumed**; I therefore expect category P below (premium present, not capturable) to be at least as likely as E. The owner can read the nine actual swap tables; they are a disclosed input to the FINAL report and a pre-FINAL economic check (section 9), never a DEV selection input.
* Not modelled: lot rounding, margin and leverage limits (the scale is capped at 4, so gross notional can reach 8 times equity), FTMO drawdown rules, weekend/holiday value-date shifts, funding of the collateral, negative-rate pass-through by the broker.

## 6. Nulls, inference and the decision rule (fixed)

* **Null A (primary), label permutation of the ranking within month blocks, return panel preserved.** The monthly weight vector (over the ten currencies, USD included) is relabelled by a uniform random permutation, **one permutation per block of 12 consecutive months, independent across blocks**; spot returns, accruals (which stay attached to the true currency, so a wrongly labelled long position earns the wrong currency's real rate), spreads, sizing (recomputed from the permuted weights) and costs are recomputed at the original dates. Within a block the persistence, dispersion and turnover of the ranking are kept; the link between the ranking and the currencies is removed. Uniform permutations are exchangeable (E[weight on a currency] = 0); a derangement would not be (it leaks -w/9). M = 5,000 replicates, seed 20261104, minimum p = 1/5,001. `p_perm12 = (1 + #{null SR >= observed SR}) / (1 + M)` on the S0 Sharpe. The same permutation is applied to all four constructions in a replicate.
* **Null A' (added by revision 2, the whole-sample relabelling).** The same relabelling of the weight vector, but **one uniform permutation of the live currencies for the entire sample** (seed 20261106, M = 5,000; `block = Kp` in `null_distribution`, tested as one permutation in every month). Reason (review M3): the real weights are persistent (the ranking is unchanged in about 180 of 221 months), so when currency returns have multi-year drift the real Sharpe has a larger sampling sd than a block-permuted null; the full-sample relabelling keeps the persistence and restores nominal size in those worlds at a small cost in power (section 8). **`p_G1 = max(p_perm12, p_permfull)`** is the p-value used by G1a (reported as `p_perm`).
* **Null B (secondary, the "sign-free exposure" null): random sign per block.** The whole weight vector is multiplied by +1 or -1, one draw per block of 12 months; gross exposure, rankings and sizing are kept, only the direction is random. M = 5,000, seed 20261105. Known imperfection: a sign change at a block edge adds turnover, so the null cost is slightly higher than the real cost (liberal); tested that null turnover is not lower.
* **Multiple testing:** Holm over the K = 3 candidates on `p_G1` (the larger of the two relabelling p-values), family alpha 0.05 (G1a). The control M has its own p-values and is outside the family. All nulls are one-sided; **there is no category for a significantly negative result (reverse carry)**: a significantly negative Sharpe is reported as a number and falls in category N.
* **What the permutation nulls do not cover (stated, not fixable by them):** (a) persistent currency-specific drift (variance ratio above 1) inflates both nulls A and B in proportion to the persistence; null A' removes most of it, null A at block 12 does not; (b) **a common carry-crash exposure** (jumps that hit high-yield currencies together, loading on the rate differential) is a systematic exposure of the real portfolio that no relabelling of currencies reproduces: in the review's crash world the registered tests rejected a true-zero premium in 11 to 13 % of panels at nominal 5 %, and category P is then overstated by roughly a factor of 2. Category E is protected by the S1 gates (false E at most 4 % in the worst world the review built; the calibration in section 8 repeats it).
* **Bootstrap (reporting only, no gate uses it):** circular block bootstrap of the monthly series, blocks of 3, 6 and 12 months, B = 5,000, seed 20261103, shared resampled months across series; the interval with the lowest lower bound is quoted. Reported: S0, S1, S2 Sharpe with intervals, the accrual and spot parts, the difference to BL, per-year and per-era tables, leave-one-currency-out, skew, worst month and maximum drawdown (carry is negatively skewed; the Sharpe flatters it).
* **Acknowledgement of multiple looks:** this arm sits inside a campaign that has already made several looks at overlapping price data, and GAMEPLAN allows at most two FINAL candidates in total at alpha 0.025, of which this arm would use one; the campaign-wide error is larger than 0.05 and is stated in the report, not corrected.

## 7. Gates, decision table, selection (fixed)

A family variant is **eligible** only if ALL hold on the single DEV run (S1 = 1.5 % mark-up unless stated):
* **G1a** Holm over the three `p_G1` (the larger of the block-12 and whole-sample relabelling p-values) rejects the variant at 0.05. **G1b** `p_flip <= 0.05` (Null B, unadjusted: a robustness requirement on the same hypothesis).
* **G2** SR(S1) > 0, with 2008-09 included.
* **G3a** SR(S1) > 0 after removing the three best calendar years by S1 net return (the ranking of years includes the partial 2021, which can be removed if it is among the three best). **G3b** S1 net return positive in at least **13 of the 18** calendar years 2003-2020 (a coin flip passes with probability about 0.05; 2021 is partial and reported only). **G3c** SR(S1) > 0 in at least 2 of the 3 eras. **G3d** SR(S1) > 0 without 2008 and 2009 (the crash and its rebound; this gate is easier to pass than G2 and is what the owner asked for).
* **G4a** SR(S1) > 0 after dropping each single non-USD currency in turn (all nine point estimates positive; the portfolio is re-formed on the remaining nine). **G4b** SR(S1) > 0 without NOK and SEK (re-formed on eight).
* **G5** materiality SR(S1) >= 0.30.

**Selection:** among eligible variants take the highest SR(S1); it is the frozen candidate, written to `research/output/fx_carry_dev/frozen_candidate.json` with this file's hash. If none, no candidate and FINAL is not used.

**Decision table (category written to `summary.json`):**

| Category | Condition | What it means, in words fixed now |
|---|---|---|
| **E** | at least one variant eligible | A DEV-selected hypothesis with an optimistically biased Sharpe (selection over three variants and nine gates, a close cousin of a known anomaly), **not a validated edge**. It would need live code for monthly currency-level positions, swap handling, a margin and drawdown design and a `reviewer` pass. Report SR at S2 next to it. |
| **P** | not E; at least one variant has G1a, G1b and SR(S0) > 0 | "The registered tests reject a zero S0 premium in DEV, but the variant does not survive the S1 mark-up or the stability gates." Not capturable as tested. **Overstated when currency returns are persistent or crash-exposed (section 8): in those zero-premium worlds the registered tests reject in up to a few tens of percent of panels, so P is evidence about the registered tests as much as about carry.** |
| **U** | not E, not P; some variant has SR(S0) > 0 | "Positive but not detectable at DEV power." Explicitly **not** "carry works" and not "carry does not exist". |
| **N** | no family variant has SR(S0) > 0 | "No carry premium observed in DEV." |

**U and N are not distinguishable evidence:** with a true premium of zero the calibration puts about two thirds of panels in U and one third in N (the split is the sign of the largest of three noisy Sharpes). Both mean "no detectable premium"; the signs of the three S0 Sharpes are reported. The lag-2 line (section 2) is reported beside the category.

## 8. Power and calibration (computed before any real run; thresholds are NOT tuned from it)

**Analytic (plug-in normal approximation for an annualised Sharpe from monthly data, `python -m research.fx_carry power`):** SE(SR) = sqrt((1 + SR^2/24) f / T), T in years, f the variance inflation (1.0 and 1.3 for autocorrelation and skew), true Sharpe = the S0 Sharpe; T = 221/12 = 18.4 years in DEV, 47/12 = 3.9 in FINAL. **Corrected in revision 2:** the first version used (1 + SR^2/2), the formula for a per-period Sharpe; with monthly data and an annualised Sharpe the term is SR^2/24 (checked against the simulated sd of the annualised Sharpe of 221 iid months, test `test_analytic_power_se_matches_simulation`). The earlier table overstated the SE and so understated power.

| Window | one-sided alpha | power at true SR 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 | SR for 80 % power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DEV, single test | 0.05 | 0.36 / 0.30 | 0.69 / 0.59 | 0.94 / 0.87 | 0.99 / 0.98 | 0.58 / 0.67 |
| DEV, first Holm step (K=3) | 0.01667 | 0.20 / 0.16 | 0.50 / 0.40 | 0.85 / 0.75 | 0.98 / 0.94 | 0.70 / 0.80 |
| FINAL, one candidate | 0.025 | 0.09 / 0.07 | 0.16 / 0.14 | 0.31 / 0.25 | 0.49 / 0.40 | 1.48 / 1.71 |

(Earlier, wrong-formula values for comparison: DEV single test 80 % power at 0.64 / 0.75, first Holm step 0.79 / 0.95, FINAL "not reached".)

**Honest reading.** 18 years of monthly data cannot resolve a Sharpe below about 0.6 to 0.8 (single test 0.58 / 0.67, first Holm step 0.70 / 0.80 at f = 1.0 / 1.3). The long-run G10 carry Sharpe in the literature (about 0.4 to 0.7 before costs, unverified here) is at or below the minimum detectable level, and on these rates the accrual alone is worth S0 Sharpe of about 0.5 at a 10 % volatility target, concentrated in 2003-2008. Reading of the calibration (a check of the machinery and of the gates, not an estimate of carry):
* **The S1 mark-up costs about 0.40 of Sharpe for V1 and 0.42 for V2** (S0 minus S1 Sharpe at every planted strength). Planted carry at the size of the interbank accrual (lambda = 0, S0 Sharpe about 0.5) is found by the Holm step (G1a, larger-p rule) in about half of the panels, reaches eligibility in only 10 %, ends in category P in 41 % and U in 47 %. A premium of S0 Sharpe 0.3 is eligible in 4 % of panels; 0.7 in 35 %; 1.0 in 67 %. **Category P or U is the expected outcome for a carry premium of the size usually quoted**; category E essentially needs a realised S0 Sharpe above about 0.8.
* **Cost of the whole-sample relabelling:** at lambda = 0 the Holm rejection falls from 0.60 (block-12 only, the earlier calibration) to 0.51, at lambda = -0.5 from 0.90 to 0.80; eligibility is unchanged (10 %, 35 %, 67 % at lambda = 0, -0.5, -1.0) because the S1 gates, not G1a, are binding there.
* **False positives are controlled only when currency returns are iid Gaussian** (row lambda = +1 in (a): G1a 4 %, no eligible panel, V1 single tests 5 to 7 %). They are **not** controlled in the other worlds: with slow currency drift the block-12 relabelling alone over-rejects (V1 single test 8 % at sd 2 %, 11 % at sd 4 %, nominal 5 %), the whole-sample relabelling removes most of that (7 % and 5 %), the sign-flip null (G1b) does not (5 % and 12 %), and the registered G1a on the larger p stays near nominal for the Holm step (3 % and 13 % for any-of-three, category P 2 % and 10 %; the 13 % at sd 4 % is well above the nominal 5 %). The carry-crash world over-rejects the single tests (9 % block-12, 11 % whole-sample, 9 % sign flip) and the registered G1a any-of-three is 5 %, category P 4 %; the review's own, harsher crash world gave 11 to 13 % for the single tests and P about 9 %. **A common crash exposure is a systematic risk factor of the real portfolio that no relabelling of currencies reproduces, so the permutation nulls cannot be made valid against it; P is then overstated by up to about a factor of 2, and this is a limitation, not something this design corrects.** The whole-sample relabelling corrects persistence, not crashes.
* **Category E is protected by the S1 gates in every zero-premium world tried** (false E at most 3 % in the worst: slow drift sd 4 %; 1 % in the crash world; 0 % in iid noise).
* **The control M is not clean** in persistent worlds: it passes its null in 50 % of panels when the currency drift has sd 4 % (a persistent drift is also a trend that 12-1 momentum picks up) and in 12 % at sd 2 %; and when the planted carry is very large (lambda <= -1: 13 % and 40 % pass). That is why M never changes a category (section 3).
* Not calibrated: fat tails other than the crash world, the real 2008 episode, regime changes in dispersion beyond the real rates themselves, so the real false-negative rate for a premium that arrives as rare crashes is higher than shown.

* **CONFIRMED** = ALL of: (i) the one-sided 97.5 % lower bound of the S1 Sharpe (2.5th percentile of the circular block bootstrap, blocks 3 and 6, the lower of the two) > 0; (ii) `p_G1` (the larger of the block-12 and the whole-sample relabelling p-values on the S0 Sharpe, 5,000 replicates each) <= 0.025; (iii) the S1 point Sharpe is > 0 when each of the nine non-USD currencies is dropped in turn (not a one-currency trade, in particular not JPY); (iv) the S1 point Sharpe is > 0 when USD is left out of the ranking (the nine foreign currencies ranked among themselves, no net USD exposure; the code path exists and is tested as a DEV sensitivity); (v) the owner's actual swap tables (section 10) give a break-even mark-up above the actual mark-up on the DEV positions (checked before the slot is spent, not after).
  **Exhaustive outcome list (implemented as `final_outcome`, tested over all 2 x 32 combinations of the sign of the S1 point Sharpe and the five conditions):** **NOT REPLICATED** = the S1 point Sharpe is <= 0 (whatever else holds; a positive lower bound with a non-positive point estimate cannot occur). Otherwise **CONFIRMED** = all of (i) to (v) hold, and **CONSISTENT BUT NOT CONFIRMED** = the S1 point Sharpe is > 0 and **not all of (i) to (v) hold** (for example a positive lower bound that turns negative when JPY is dropped). The report lists which of (i) to (v) failed. With 47 months the interval is very wide (SE of the Sharpe about 0.5) and is said to be.
* A CONFIRMED counts as one of the two FINAL slots at alpha 0.025; the owner allocates the slots across arms (this one, the ATR-floor study, the multi-asset trend study) before any FINAL run.

## 11. Limitations and an optional follow-up (not a dependency)

* **The accrual input is a 3-month interbank monthly average, not an overnight or policy rate.** It contains a term component and a bank-credit component, the latter large and different by country in 2007-2012; it also smooths turning points (`carry_data_notes.md` section 1). A cleaner series (central-bank policy rate or overnight call rate) would change the accrual by tens of basis points in most years and by more in 2008-2012, and could change a few rankings when two currencies are within that distance. I expect it not to change the verdict category unless the result is marginal; I cannot show that without running it. **Optional follow-up only:** after the DEV result, if the owner wants, such a series could be downloaded and the accrual re-measured as a descriptive sensitivity (it would not change eligibility); using it as the primary series would need a pre-run revision and a new hash. The study stays with the 3-month series as registered.
* **Policy-rate follow-up declined for now (2026-11-03).** The review notes that the 3-month series embeds expected rate changes and bank-credit premia (2007-2012), which inflate the S0 accrual for currencies in hiking or stress periods, and that any later use of a cleaner series after the result is a second look that will be read. It stays a limitation: the 3-month series is the registered accrual.
* **U and N are nearly indistinguishable and V3 and M are not blind** (sections 0 and 7). Only category E is a candidate; only E is protected by the S1 gates against the persistent-drift and crash worlds.
* **Net USD exposure.** USD is in the ranking and sits in the long or short set in a majority of decisions (rates only: long 25 %, short 39 % of decisions), so the portfolios carry a sizeable net USD position; `mean_net_USD_weight` and `share_months_net_long_USD` are reported beside BL, and the USD-left-out-of-the-ranking sensitivity (which is FINAL condition (iv)) is produced in DEV so the owner sees it before the slot decision.
* The mark-up is a stress, not a measurement (one swap table seen, AUDUSD; today's snapshot, not history). The spread floors are assumptions. Both are small or decisive depending on the mark-up, which the owner can read.
* NOK enters the panel only in February 2007, so the cross-section is nine currencies for 22 % of DEV; the early years are the high-dispersion ones, where NOK (often a high-yield currency then) is absent from the long side.
* Survivorship in the currency choice; G10 only; 2008 and the post-2021 period are known to me; the DEV period contains one carry crash and a long low-dispersion stretch, so any DEV Sharpe is a few-episode statistic.
